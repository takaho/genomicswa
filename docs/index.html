Style map

Compare versions of a scientific paragraph — for example before and after applying a SKILL.md — and see how their position changes among published life-science papers of a chosen field.

Everything runs in your browser. Your text is not sent anywhere. This is a descriptive tool: it shows positions relative to published papers; it does not identify fields, authors, countries or the use of AI, and it makes no claim of accuracy.

About this tool

What it measures. 28 syntactic and rhetorical features per text: sentence and word length, lexical diversity (MATTR), what the clause subjects are (we, data/findings, methods, patients, organisms, genes/cells/molecules, pronouns, "It is/There are"), passive voice, hedges and boosters, past tense, rhetorical moves (aim, novelty, importance, limitation, future work), connectives and nominalisations. Rates are per 1,000 words; subject types and passive voice are shares of clauses.

Reference. 53,959 introduction and discussion sections of PubMed Central open-access research articles from 90 OpenAlex life- and health-science subfields (about 150 per field, period and section; 2019–22 and 2024–25), measured with exactly the same code as your text. The style map is a principal-component analysis of the 90 fields' 2019–22 medians. The two directions are average differences in standardised feature space, within fields.

How the features are computed. In the browser with wink-nlp (tokens, parts of speech, lemmas) and word lists. Clause subjects, passive voice and tense are approximated with part-of-speech patterns. Agreement with the spaCy-based features of the research corpus (Pearson r on 14,400 sections) is listed below; features marked * (r < 0.7) are shown but not used for the map.

Limits. Short texts (< 300 words) give unstable values. Positions describe how typical the prose is; they say nothing about quality, correctness or who wrote it. The region labels are averages of many papers and must not be read as statements about any author.

Source. Endo T. Disciplinary prose styles in the life sciences (manuscript in preparation, 2026). Code, SKILL files and data: github.com/takaho/genomicswa (code MIT, SKILL files CC BY 4.0). Example texts are CC BY articles cited by PMCID.
