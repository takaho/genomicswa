# Clinical Biochemistry: research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Clinical Biochemistry article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Clinical Biochemistry articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.70). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "However, X…" → "X…" where no contrast is meant. Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): aim statements is less common in Clinical Biochemistry. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.4 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Booster adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: clearly, indeed, strongly, markedly, dramatically, remarkably. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Clinical Biochemistry. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Clinical Biochemistry (use where it fits): "Indeed we …"; "Further studies …"; "At least …"; "Moreover we …"; "Two of …"

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Clinical Biochemistry articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.3 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Clinical Biochemistry introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.7 per 300 words; importance words about 0.9 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.1 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.7 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.67 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Clinical Biochemistry discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.7 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.4 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.4 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.49 characters per word.
