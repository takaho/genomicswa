# Gastroenterology: research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Gastroenterology article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Gastroenterology articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical introduction phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits): "Therefore we …"; "The aim …"; "Previous studies …"; "Several studies …"; "We therefore …"; "It can …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Gastroenterology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits): "In conclusion …"; "Although there …"; "First this …"; "In a …"; "First it …"; "Second this …"

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Gastroenterology articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.3 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Gastroenterology introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.8 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.7 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.9 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.67 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Gastroenterology discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.7 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.2 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.47 characters per word.

## Style illustration (Gastroenterology, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants); verbatim, CC BY; do not copy content)

Both these posttranslational modifications may contribute to CeD pathogenesis [ ]. The recognition motif for the TG2-mediated deamidation is QXP [ ], which is present in the majority of the gliadin sequences recognized by T and B cells [ ]. Since deamidation of these prolamin peptides further improves their fitting to the antigen-binding pocket of the CeD-associated HLA-DQ molecules [ ], this process may exponentially amplify the immune response."
- Discussion (PMC9786662, Neurogastroenterology & Motility, 2022): "In this work we describe that components of fecal supernatants, a proxy for the intestinal microenvironment, regulate gene expression of colonoids. Hence, the microbiota and metabolite profiles of fecal supernatants from healthy subjects and IBS patients with predominant diarrhea differed substantially and induced distinct regulation of colonoid gene expression. This suggests that our experimental set‐up, adding patient‐derived fecal supernatants to colonoids established from a healthy donor, has the potential to facilitate studies of intestinal barrier cross talk and explore the impact of the intestinal microenvironment on the pathophysiology of IBS."
