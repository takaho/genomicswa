# Aging: research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Aging article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Aging articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: replace "It is known that…"/"There are…" with the entity as subject. Example: "It is known that X regulates Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Additive connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "X… Y…" → "X… In addition, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent. Example: "X was measured in all samples" → "We measured X in all samples". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Aging; patients/participants as subject is less common in Aging. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Aging (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "We found …"; "We show …"; "Our results …"; "We also …"; "These findings …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 2.9 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.4 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: clearly, indeed, strongly, markedly, dramatically, remarkably. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.70 (all fields MATTR about 0.69). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "X causes Y" → "These data suggest that X causes Y". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Aging; patients/participants as subject is less common in Aging; limitation statements is less common in Aging. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Aging (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "Our data …"; "It will …"; "We propose …"; "We found …"; "On the …"

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Aging articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.4 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Aging introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.8 per 300 words; importance words about 1.2 per 300 words; nominalisations about 19.6 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.68 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Aging discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.9 per 300 words; nominalisations about 19.5 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.9 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.1 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.55 characters per word.
