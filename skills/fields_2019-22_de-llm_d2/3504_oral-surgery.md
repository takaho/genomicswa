# Oral Surgery: research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Oral Surgery article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Oral Surgery articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). - **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 20.5 per 300 words (all fields about 19.0 per 300 words). - **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "Moreover, X…" → "X…". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).

- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Oral Surgery. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Summary connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Example: "Collectively, these…" → "These…". Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported"). Example: "We measured X in all samples" → "X was measured in all samples". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.40 characters per word (all fields about 5.49 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions. Example: "X is known to regulate Y" → "It is known that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Oral Surgery (use where it fits): "Within the …"; "In the …"; "The present …"; "This can …"; "The results …"; "It can …"; "…within the limitations…"; "…within the limitations of…"

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Oral Surgery articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.3 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Oral Surgery introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 1.1 per 300 words; importance words about 0.8 per 300 words; nominalisations about 20.5 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.7 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.63 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Oral Surgery discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 1.0 per 300 words; importance words about 0.5 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.6 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.40 characters per word.
