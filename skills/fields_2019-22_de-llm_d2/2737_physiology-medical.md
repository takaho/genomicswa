# Physiology (medical): research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Physiology (medical) article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Physiology (medical) articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X explains Y" → "X may explain Y". Apply in several places.
- **Additive connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "X… Y…" → "X… In addition, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 20.2 per 300 words (all fields about 19.0 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits): "In addition …"; "However whether …"; "Therefore we …"; "Some studies …"; "Previous studies …"; "Studies have …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "X causes Y" → "These data suggest that X causes Y". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits): "Previous studies …"; "This effect …"; "On the …"; "Therefore the …"; "In addition …"; "Furthermore it …"

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Physiology (medical) articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.3 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Physiology (medical) introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 1.0 per 300 words; importance words about 1.0 per 300 words; nominalisations about 20.2 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.67 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Physiology (medical) discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.7 per 300 words; nominalisations about 19.5 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.50 characters per word.

## Style illustration (Physiology (medical), 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants); verbatim, CC BY; do not copy content)
- Introduction (PMC8175573, Communications Biology, 2021): "A chemical compound screen is a comprehensive and direct approach to discover new regulators or pathways in target cells. In this study, we employed an imaging-based high-throughput screening system, combined with two distinct DNA dyes to capture morphological changes of the immortalized erythroid cell lines. Among >3300 chemical compounds, we identified multiple histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors (HDACi) that largely increased the frequency of enucleated cells. Gene expression profiling revealed that HDACi treatment led to the induction of various cytoskeletal genes including kinesins."
- Discussion (PMC6406767, Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2019): "To the best of our knowledge, only one study has prospectively determined the relationship between relative strength and falls risk, and they reported that in community-dwelling older women, low relative strength was associated with a higher incidence of falls [ ]. The aforementioned study by Gadelha et al. calculated relative strength using a ratio of strength to muscle CSA; therefore, it is unclear whether low relative strength measured by muscle strength to mass ratio (which was used in our study) also increases falls risk."
