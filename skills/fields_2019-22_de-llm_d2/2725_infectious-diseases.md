# Infectious Diseases: research-article prose style, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Infectious Diseases article of 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants). Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Infectious Diseases articles (2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants)) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 2.8 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.3 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.58 characters per word (all fields about 5.66 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).

## Remove AI-polishing style
Recent text often carries vocabulary and habits that became common after 2023. In Infectious Diseases articles of 2019–2022:
- Words such as additionally, notably, crucial, pivotal, robust, comprehensive, insights, landscape, interplay, enhance, elucidate, underscore, showcase, foster, harness, delve, intricate, multifaceted occurred about 0.3 times per 300 words in total. Replace them with plain wording, for example: additionally → in addition / also; notably → (delete) / in particular; crucial / pivotal → important / key, or state the role; robust → strong / consistent; insights → information / understanding; enhance → increase / improve; elucidate → determine / define; underscore / highlight → indicate / show; landscape → profile / range; interplay → interaction.
- Restore explicit causal links where the reasoning is causal: "therefore", "thus", "because" (these were about one third more frequent before 2023).
- Prefer verbs to abstract nouns and plain words to long Latinate ones ("X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X increases when Y is inhibited").
- Use "we" for what the authors did and found, rather than "This study/These findings" as subjects.
- Keep established methods and findings in the passive where the field used it ("X was measured", "Y has been reported").
- Avoid stacking importance words (crucial, key, essential, pivotal) and "highlight/underscore/reveal".
- 2019–2022 Infectious Diseases introduction targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.7 per 300 words; importance words about 0.9 per 300 words; nominalisations about 18.5 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.4 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.58 characters per word.
- 2019–2022 Infectious Diseases discussion targets for these habits: causal connectives about 0.9 per 300 words; importance words about 0.8 per 300 words; nominalisations about 19.1 per 300 words; "we" as subject about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects; findings/data/study as subject about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects; passive about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects; word length about 5.46 characters per word.

## Style illustration (Infectious Diseases, 2019–2022 (before AI writing assistants); verbatim, CC BY; do not copy content)
- Introduction (PMC7285287, Microorganisms, 2020): "Here, we report a screen of the Pharmakon 1600 (MicroSource Discovery Systems, Inc.) library containing 1600 clinically tested drug compounds for those with C. albicans antibiofilm activity. This screen differs from the two previously reported screens of this library in three important aspects [ ]. First, our primary screen and validating secondary screen focused on the ability of the compounds to prevent biofilm formation (inhibition), and an additional secondary screen focused on testing those initial “hits” for their abilities to disrupt mature biofilms."
- Discussion (PMC9505348, Vaccines, 2022): "The COVID-19 pandemic has strongly affected cancer patients. It is known that the diagnosis of cancer is related to increased mortality from SARS-CoV-2 infection. In parallel, delays in cancer treatment and follow-ups due to restrictions imposed during the COVID-19 pandemic negatively affected survival and increased the risk of late diagnosis of the disease and its progression [ ]. Therefore, a vaccination schedule that successfully protects cancer patients was of great importance."
