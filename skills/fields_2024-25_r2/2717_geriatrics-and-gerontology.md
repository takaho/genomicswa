# Geriatrics and Gerontology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Geriatrics and Gerontology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Geriatrics and Gerontology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms. Example: keep one term for one concept: do not alternate "rise", "elevation", "increase". Apply in several places.
- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Example: "…" → "Taken together, …". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive. Example: "We found that X increased" → "X increased". Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent. Example: "X was measured in all samples" → "We measured X in all samples". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is involved" → "X is likely involved". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Geriatrics and Gerontology; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Geriatrics and Gerontology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity. Example: "X binds Y" → "We found that X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects. Example: "X binds Y. The binding of X to Y…" → "X binds Y. This…". Apply in several places.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight. Example: "These results highlight/reveal that X…" → "These results indicate that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is involved" → "X is likely involved". Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Geriatrics and Gerontology; limitation statements is more common in Geriatrics and Gerontology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Geriatrics and Gerontology (use where it fits): "This study …"; "In such …"; "Our findings …"; "Data from …"; "It may …"; "Other studies …"
