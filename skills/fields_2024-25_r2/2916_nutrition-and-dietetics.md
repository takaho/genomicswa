# Nutrition and Dietetics: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Nutrition and Dietetics article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Nutrition and Dietetics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive. Example: "We found that X increased" → "X increased". Apply in several places.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.74 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Nutrition and Dietetics; method/model/approach as subject is less common in Nutrition and Dietetics. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Nutrition and Dietetics (use where it fits): "A study …"; "Other studies …"; "Previous research …"; "This study …"; "Therefore this …"; "In a …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive. Example: "We found that X increased" → "X increased". Apply in several places.
