# Hematology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Hematology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Hematology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Hematology; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Hematology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Hematology (use where it fits): "We previously …"; "The aim …"; "Here we …"; "Our previous …"; "Moreover the …"; "It also …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Example: "…" → "Taken together, …". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Hematology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Hematology (use where it fits): "These data …"; "In our …"; "Our data …"; "The addition …"; "The limitations …"; "Nevertheless our …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, insights, robust, pivotal, landscape, enhances) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Hematology; do not exceed that, and do not add them where the original has none.
