# Physiology (medical): research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Physiology (medical) article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Physiology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Additive connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "X… Y…" → "X… In addition, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X explains Y" → "X may explain Y". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits): "Therefore the …"; "One of …"; "Therefore this …"; "Some studies …"; "It is …"; "Therefore we …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.

Typical discussion phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits): "A previous …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "Although no …"; "Moreover we …"; "Indeed in …"; "Finally this …"
