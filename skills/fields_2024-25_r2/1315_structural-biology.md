# Structural Biology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Structural Biology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Structural Biology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…"). Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.67 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X may explain Y" → "X explains Y" (only where the excerpt states it as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.0 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Structural Biology; patients/participants as subject is less common in Structural Biology; aim statements is less common in Structural Biology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.
