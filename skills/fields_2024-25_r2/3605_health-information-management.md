# Health Information Management: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Health Information Management article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Health Information Management articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight. Example: "These results highlight/reveal that X…" → "These results indicate that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Health Information Management; future-work statements is more common in Health Information Management; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Health Information Management. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent. Example: "X was measured in all samples" → "We measured X in all samples". Apply in several places.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "However, X…" → "X…" where no contrast is meant. Apply in several places.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Health Information Management; future-work statements is more common in Health Information Management; limitation statements is more common in Health Information Management. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Health Information Management (use where it fits): "Future work …"; "This research …"; "To address …"; "This study …"; "Limitations and …"; "The research …"; "…data such as…"
