# Pharmacology (medical): research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Pharmacology (medical) article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Pharmacology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.1 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Example: "We observed that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.0 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Example: "Previous studies show that X regulates Y" → "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmacology (medical) (use where it fits): "The most …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "In this …"; "Moreover it …"; "In a …"; "Most of …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "X causes Y" → "These data suggest that X causes Y". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmacology (medical) (use where it fits): "In particular …"; "Thus these …"; "They are …"; "Our results …"; "These limitations …"; "In previous …"
