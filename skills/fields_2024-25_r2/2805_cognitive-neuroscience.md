# Cognitive Neuroscience: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Cognitive Neuroscience article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Cognitive Neuroscience articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

Over infinitesimal time delays, the autoregressive matrix approaches the identity and the error approaches zero. On the other hand, for infinitely long delays the autoregressive matrix approaches zero and the prediction error approaches the variance of the data (R2 = 0)."
