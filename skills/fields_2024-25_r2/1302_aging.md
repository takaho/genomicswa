# Aging: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Aging article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Aging articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Booster adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: clearly, indeed, strongly, markedly, dramatically, remarkably. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is likely involved" → "X is involved" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: replace "It is known that…"/"There are…" with the entity as subject. Example: "It is known that X regulates Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Aging; patients/participants as subject is less common in Aging. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Aging (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "Our findings …"; "We also …"; "We found …"; "We further …"; "These findings …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.0 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "X causes Y" → "These data suggest that X causes Y". Apply in several places.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent. Example: "X was measured in all samples" → "We measured X in all samples". Apply in several places.
- **Booster adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: clearly, indeed, strongly, markedly, dramatically, remarkably. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Aging; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Aging; limitation statements is less common in Aging. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Aging (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "It will …"; "Our data …"; "We propose …"; "We found …"; "We also …"
