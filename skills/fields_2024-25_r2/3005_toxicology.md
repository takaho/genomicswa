# Toxicology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Toxicology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Toxicology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Summary connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Example: "Collectively, these…" → "These…". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Toxicology (use where it fits): "The most …"; "The study …"; "Since the …"; "While the …"; "To the …"; "As the …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.78 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases. Example: "X goes up when Y is blocked" → "X increases upon inhibition of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.72 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.
- **Summary connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is involved" → "X is likely involved". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Toxicology (use where it fits): "However more …"; "As for …"; "There have …"; "Our analysis …"; "In particular …"; "For these …"
