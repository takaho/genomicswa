# Small Animals: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Small Animals article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Small Animals articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X plays a crucial role in Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight. Example: "These results highlight/reveal that X…" → "These results indicate that X…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Small Animals; patients/participants as subject is less common in Small Animals; method/model/approach as subject is more common in Small Animals.
