Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Oral Surgery article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Oral Surgery articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions. Example: "X is known to regulate Y" → "It is known that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence. Example: "X, which was measured in all samples and which increased with age, was associated with Y." → "X was measured in all samples. It increased with age and was associated with Y.". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): limitation statements is more common in Oral Surgery; method/model/approach as subject is more common in Oral Surgery; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Oral Surgery. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 23 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is likely involved" → "X is involved" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Summary connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion. Example: "Collectively, these…" → "These…". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Example: "Previous studies show that X regulates Y" → "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Oral Surgery. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Oral Surgery (use where it fits): "Within the …"; "The present …"; "In the …"; "This result …"; "This can …"; "The results …"; "…within the limitations of…"; "…within the limitations…"
