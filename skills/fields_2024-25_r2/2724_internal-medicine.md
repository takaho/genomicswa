# Internal Medicine: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Internal Medicine article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Internal Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.0 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Example: "Previous studies show that X regulates Y" → "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "Moreover, X…" → "X…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): aim statements is more common in Internal Medicine; patients/participants as subject is more common in Internal Medicine; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Internal Medicine. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Internal Medicine (use where it fits): "However there …"; "Several studies …"; "The aim …"; "In a …"; "This study …"; "Therefore we …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.7 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Example: "Previous studies show that X regulates Y" → "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y". Apply in several places.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.60 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is involved" → "X is likely involved". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Internal Medicine; limitation statements is more common in Internal Medicine. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Internal Medicine (use where it fits): "Study limitations …"; "To our …"; "Our study …"; "In our …"; "In a …"; "We were …"
