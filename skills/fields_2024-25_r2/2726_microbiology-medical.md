# Microbiology (medical): research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Microbiology (medical) article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Microbiology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 6.01 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases. Example: "X goes up when Y is blocked" → "X increases upon inhibition of Y". Apply in several places.

How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "However, X…" → "X…" where no contrast is meant. Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Microbiology (medical); patients/participants as subject is less common in Microbiology (medical). Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.
