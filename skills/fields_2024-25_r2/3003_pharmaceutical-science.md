# Pharmaceutical Science: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Pharmaceutical Science article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Pharmaceutical Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Pharmaceutical Science; method/model/approach as subject is more common in Pharmaceutical Science; aim statements is less common in Pharmaceutical Science. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmaceutical Science (use where it fits): "Additionally the …"; "Finally the …"; "It has …"; "In this …"; "It is …"; "In addition …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X may explain Y" → "X explains Y" (only where the excerpt states it as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Example: "We observed that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 5.0 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they. Example: "X binds Y. This…" → "X binds Y. The binding of X to Y…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Pharmaceutical Science; method/model/approach as subject is more common in Pharmaceutical Science; limitation statements is less common in Pharmaceutical Science. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmaceutical Science (use where it fits): "The in …"; "The results …"; "As shown …"; "All the …"; "Overall this …"; "This work …"
