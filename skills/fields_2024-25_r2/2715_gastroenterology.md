# Gastroenterology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Gastroenterology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Gastroenterology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical introduction phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits): "Therefore we …"; "Although the …"; "Notably the …"; "Therefore there …"; "This study …"; "Given the …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity. Example: "X binds Y" → "We found that X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions. Example: "X is known to regulate Y" → "It is known that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is more common in Gastroenterology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits): "In our …"; "First this …"; "To our …"; "In conclusion …"; "There was …"; "Our study …"
