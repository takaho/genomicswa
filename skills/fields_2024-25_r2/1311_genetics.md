# Genetics: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Genetics article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Genetics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…"). Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is more common in Genetics. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Genetics (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "In most …"; "In contrast …"; "In this …"; "Our results …"; "Finally we …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Genetics. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Genetics (use where it fits): "Overall we …"; "Previous work …"; "To explore …"; "To investigate …"; "Here we …"; "In previous …"; "…evidence for a…"
