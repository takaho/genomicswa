# Pharmacology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Pharmacology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Pharmacology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.9 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Example: "We observed that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmacology (use where it fits): "Therefore in …"; "It is …"; "Therefore we …"; "In our …"; "Thus the …"; "Previous studies …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they. Example: "X binds Y. This…" → "X binds Y. The binding of X to Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported"). Example: "We measured X in all samples" → "X was measured in all samples". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.70 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms. Example: keep one term for one concept: do not alternate "rise", "elevation", "increase". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): limitation statements is less common in Pharmacology; patients/participants as subject is less common in Pharmacology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmacology (use where it fits): "To further …"; "As shown …"; "The effect …"; "Studies have …"; "In addition …"; "As a …"
