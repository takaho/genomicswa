## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 17.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X plays a crucial role in Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.73 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they.
- Context only (do not force): organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Pathology and Forensic Medicine. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.
