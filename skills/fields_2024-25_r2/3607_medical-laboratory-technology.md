# Medical Laboratory Technology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Medical Laboratory Technology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Medical Laboratory Technology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise. Example: "Moreover, X…" → "X…". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Medical Laboratory Technology; gap/novelty statements is less common in Medical Laboratory Technology; organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject is less common in Medical Laboratory Technology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Medical Laboratory Technology (use where it fits): "However this …"; "The aim …"; "However these …"; "In this …"; "This is …"; "Hence the …"; "…of the results…"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.57 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions. Example: "X is known to regulate Y" → "It is known that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): method/model/approach as subject is more common in Medical Laboratory Technology; gap/novelty statements is less common in Medical Laboratory Technology; patients/participants as subject is less common in Medical Laboratory Technology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Medical Laboratory Technology (use where it fits): "For the …"; "This can …"; "In such …"; "If the …"; "Overall the …"; "There is …"; "…of the new…"
