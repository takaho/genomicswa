# Nephrology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Nephrology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Nephrology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).

Typical introduction phrasing in Nephrology (use where it fits): "The aim …"; "Previous studies …"; "Recently the …"; "Therefore we …"; "However the …"; "Nevertheless the …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they. Example: "X binds Y. This…" → "X binds Y. The binding of X to Y…". Apply in several places.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence. Example: "X, which was measured in all samples and which increased with age, was associated with Y." → "X was measured in all samples. It increased with age and was associated with Y.". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Nephrology (use where it fits): "Our study …"; "There may …"; "In our …"; "In a …"; "The possible …"; "Additionally a …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, robust, pivotal, enhancing, elucidate) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Nephrology; do not exceed that, and do not add them where the original has none.
