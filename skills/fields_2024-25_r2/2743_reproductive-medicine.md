# Reproductive Medicine: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Reproductive Medicine article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Reproductive Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Example: "We observed that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they. Example: "X binds Y. This…" → "X binds Y. The binding of X to Y…". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Reproductive Medicine (use where it fits): "It has …"; "During the …"; "Several studies …"; "In our …"; "Furthermore it …"; "It is …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is likely involved" → "X is involved" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.6 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "X causes Y" → "These data suggest that X causes Y". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Reproductive Medicine (use where it fits): "Studies have …"; "To the …"; "Although there …"; "It has …"; "Some studies …"; "In our …"
