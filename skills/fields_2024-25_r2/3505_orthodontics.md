Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Orthodontics article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Orthodontics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.8 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense. Do not overshoot: stop at the target.

- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.1 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
