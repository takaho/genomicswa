# Dermatology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Dermatology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Dermatology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence. Example: "X, which was measured in all samples and which increased with age, was associated with Y." → "X was measured in all samples. It increased with age and was associated with Y.". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.

Typical introduction phrasing in Dermatology (use where it fits): "This study …"; "It also …"; "It is …"; "Despite its …"; "Although the …"; "The aim …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Example: "This suggests that X causes Y" → "X causes Y" (only where stated as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 1 per 300 words).

Typical discussion phrasing in Dermatology (use where it fits): "In present …"; "The data …"; "The most …"; "In our …"; "This was …"; "In a …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, insights, enhancing, robust, elucidate, enhances) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Dermatology; do not exceed that, and do not add them where the original has none.

The conversion of PPD into N-mono and N,N-diacetylated (MAPPD and DAPPD, respectively) metabolites was mediated by N-acetyltransferase-1 (NAT-1) located in the epidermis and N-acetyltransferase-2 (NAT-2) located in the liver and gut [ ]. The conversion of aromatic amines to N-hydroxylamines by CyP450 oxidation is a crucial step in the activation of arylamines."
- Discussion (PMC10940128, JAAD International, 2024): "This study conducted a side-by-side comparison of BTX and MWT for axillary hyperhidrosis in a randomized, controlled trial with 1-year FU. Both treatments effectively improved axillary sweat, odor as well as QoL. While BTX performed objectively better than MWT for sweat reduction at 6-month FU, they performed similarly on sweat reduction at 1-year FU. In contrast, odor reduction at 6-months was similar, but at 1-year FU, odor reduction started to revert on the BTX-side, while it sustained on the MWT-side."
