# Ophthalmology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Ophthalmology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Ophthalmology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.74 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Ophthalmology (use where it fits): "At present …"; "As the …"; "It has …"; "There are …"; "This study …"; "The aim …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.57 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs. Example: "X increases upon inhibition of Y" → "X goes up when Y is blocked". Apply in several places.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X plays a crucial role in Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y"). Example: "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation" → "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow". Apply in several places.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite. Example: "X… Y…" → "X…; however, Y…". Apply in several places.

Typical discussion phrasing in Ophthalmology (use where it fits): "In our …"; "They reported …"; "In conclusion …"; "They found …"; "In previous …"; "A previous …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, insights, comprehensive, enhance, enhancing, robust, elucidate, enhances) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Ophthalmology; do not exceed that, and do not add them where the original has none.
