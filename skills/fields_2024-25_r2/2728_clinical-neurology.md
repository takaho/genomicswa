# Clinical Neurology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Clinical Neurology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Clinical Neurology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…"). Example: "These results show that X…" → "We show that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…"). Example: "X increased, suggesting…" → "These findings suggest that X…". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often. Example: "X is involved" → "X is likely involved". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences. Example: avoid repeating "increase" in neighbouring sentences: "rise", "elevation", "higher levels". Apply in several places.

Typical introduction phrasing in Clinical Neurology (use where it fits): "We also …"; "However these …"; "In this …"; "It has …"; "Finally we …"; "However to …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity. Example: "X binds Y" → "We found that X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): limitation statements is more common in Clinical Neurology; patients/participants as subject is more common in Clinical Neurology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Clinical Neurology (use where it fits): "A previous …"; "Both studies …"; "In line …"; "Our results …"; "These data …"; "We showed …"
