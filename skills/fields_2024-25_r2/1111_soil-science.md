# Soil Science: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Soil Science article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Soil Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 24.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major. Example: "X regulates Y" → "X is a key regulator of Y". Apply in several places.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects. Example: "We observed that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.70 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms. Example: keep one term for one concept: do not alternate "rise", "elevation", "increase". Apply in several places.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result. Do not overshoot: stop at the target (at most about 2 per 300 words).
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent. Example: "X was measured in all samples" → "We measured X in all samples". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Soil Science; aim statements is less common in Soil Science. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Soil Science (use where it fits): "Therefore it …"; "They found …"; "The research …"; "However the …"; "However most …"; "Moreover the …"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 23.6 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation"). Example: "Y activates X, which changes how cells grow" → "Activation of X by Y alters cell proliferation". Apply in several places.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.69 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms. Example: keep one term for one concept: do not alternate "rise", "elevation", "increase". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X may explain Y" → "X explains Y" (only where the excerpt states it as a finding). Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Soil Science; gap/novelty statements is less common in Soil Science; limitation statements is less common in Soil Science. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Soil Science (use where it fits): "Effects of …"; "This indicates …"; "This is …"; "Effect of …"; "This can …"; "The addition …"
