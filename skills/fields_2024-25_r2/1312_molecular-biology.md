# Molecular Biology: research-article prose style, 2024–2025

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a Molecular Biology article of 2024–2025. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: open-access Molecular Biology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average.

## General rules (learned from earlier rewriting runs)
- Change only the features listed below, in the direction and by the amount shown. Leave everything else as in the original.
- For features marked "Apply in several places", earlier rewrites changed too little: count the feature in the original and change it throughout the excerpt, not just once.
- Do not shorten sentences, split sentences, or turn passive clauses into active ones unless a target below asks for it; otherwise keep the original sentence length and voice.
- Do not change who or what the sentences are about (patients, animals, plants, methods): that follows from the subject matter.
- Do not add aims, limitations, novelty claims or future-work statements that the original does not contain.

## Introduction: what to change (most important first)

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…"). Do not overshoot: stop at the target.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would. Example: "X may explain Y" → "X explains Y" (only where the excerpt states it as a finding). Apply in several places.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: replace "It is known that…"/"There are…" with the entity as subject. Example: "It is known that X regulates Y" → "X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Molecular Biology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical introduction phrasing in Molecular Biology (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "In this …"; "Our results …"; "We found …"; "While these …"; "We further …"; "…are found in…"; "…role of the…"; "…of the two…"

## Discussion: what to change (most important first)

- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.1 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation. Example: "Previous studies showed that X regulated Y" → "Previous studies show that X regulates Y". Apply in several places.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study. Example: "These data indicate that X binds Y" → "X binds Y". Apply in several places.
- Context only (do not force): patients/participants as subject is less common in Molecular Biology; limitation statements is less common in Molecular Biology. Where the original already contains such content, phrase it in the field's way.

Typical discussion phrasing in Molecular Biology (use where it fits): "Here we …"; "We show …"; "Our data …"; "Our results …"; "We have …"; "We previously …"

It is responsible for the activation of several antioxidants and phase I and II detoxifying and drug-metabolizing enzymes. In the absence of stimuli, Nrf2 protein remains predominantly in the cytoplasm where it interacts with the actin-binding protein Keap1. The Nrf2/Keap1 complex facilitates the inhibition of Nrf2 through ubiquitin ligase cullin3-mediated proteolysis [ ]. When the levels of oxidants/stressors increase, Nrf2 unbinds from Keap1 and translocates into the nucleus, acting as a transcription factor."

Here, we investigate the molecular evolution the plastid-encoded photosystem genes in angiosperms. We show that hallmarks of adaptive evolution, namely positive selection and recurrent evolution, are widespread in the photosystem subunits of angiosperms."
