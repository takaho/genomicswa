# Developmental Neuroscience: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Developmental Neuroscience article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Developmental Neuroscience articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: replace "It is known that…"/"There are…" with the entity as subject.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.

Typical introduction phrasing in Developmental Neuroscience (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "In the …"; "We found …"; "However whether …"; "Our findings …"; "Taken together …"; "Our results …"; "These findings …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.4 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical discussion phrasing in Developmental Neuroscience (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "Our data …"; "Our previous …"; "We found …"; "We have …"; "We show …"; "We propose …"; "It will …"
