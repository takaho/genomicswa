# Emergency Medicine: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Emergency Medicine article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Emergency Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.69 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Aim statements: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: state the aim explicitly: "Here, we…", "In this study…", "We aimed to…", "To investigate/determine/assess…".
- **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 6.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.

Typical introduction phrasing in Emergency Medicine (use where it fits the content): "This study …"; "During the …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore this …"; "In a …"; "As such …"; "There is …"; "To our …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical discussion phrasing in Emergency Medicine (use where it fits the content): "Limitations the …"; "Limitations our …"; "Limitations this …"; "This finding …"; "This study …"; "Further research …"; "There was …"; "The study …"; "…the time to…"

## Vocabulary

The results demonstrated no significant differences between the two groups in terms of technical and clinical success rates, patient satisfaction, AEs, or recurrence. Notably, 97.06% of patients (33/34) in the ERAT group achieved complete pain relief within 2 days of treatment, a significantly higher rate than that observed in the LA group (77.94%, 53/68). This finding highlighted the ability of direct visualization ERAT to effectively and rapidly alleviate pain symptoms."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
