# Pharmacology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Pharmacology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Pharmacology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.9 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmacology (use where it fits the content): "Therefore in …"; "It is …"; "Therefore we …"; "In our …"; "Thus the …"; "Previous studies …"; "It has …"; "However its …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmacology (use where it fits the content): "To further …"; "As shown …"; "The effect …"; "Studies have …"; "In addition …"; "As a …"; "However further …"; "Similarly the …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, enhance, comprehensive, insights, enhancing, elucidate, enhances, pivotal) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Pharmacology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
