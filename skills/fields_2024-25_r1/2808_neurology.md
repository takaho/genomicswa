# Neurology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Neurology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Neurology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.90 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.8 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").

Typical introduction phrasing in Neurology (use where it fits the content): "Taken together …"; "Previous research …"; "Recent research …"; "Our results …"; "These findings …"; "Our findings …"; "Studies have …"; "While these …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.1 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").

Typical discussion phrasing in Neurology (use where it fits the content): "Future studies …"; "Previously we …"; "Here we …"; "Thus we …"; "Consistent with …"; "Our data …"; "Collectively our …"; "Our results …"; "…to the second…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, enhance, enhancing, insights, comprehensive, robust, enhances, elucidate) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Neurology; do not exceed that.

Our results revealed that LDLr−/−.Leiden mice fed a HFD and HFD+C exhibited cSVD characteristics, including reduced CBF, brain atrophy, impaired endothelial and BBB function, as well as vascular inflammation and neuroinflammation."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
