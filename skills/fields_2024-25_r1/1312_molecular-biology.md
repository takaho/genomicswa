# Molecular Biology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Molecular Biology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Molecular Biology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.

Typical introduction phrasing in Molecular Biology (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "In this …"; "Our results …"; "We found …"; "While these …"; "We further …"; "Although these …"; "Additionally we …"; "…are found in…"; "…role of the…"; "…of the two…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.1 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.

Typical discussion phrasing in Molecular Biology (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "We show …"; "Our data …"; "Our results …"; "We have …"; "We previously …"; "Recent studies …"; "The first …"

Here, we investigate the molecular evolution the plastid-encoded photosystem genes in angiosperms.
