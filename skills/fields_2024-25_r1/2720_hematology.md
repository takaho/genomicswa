# Hematology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Hematology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Hematology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Hematology (use where it fits the content): "We previously …"; "The aim …"; "Here we …"; "Our previous …"; "Moreover the …"; "It also …"; "In this …"; "In a …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").

Typical discussion phrasing in Hematology (use where it fits the content): "These data …"; "In our …"; "Our data …"; "The addition …"; "The limitations …"; "Nevertheless our …"; "Notably in …"; "We also …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, insights, robust, pivotal, landscape, enhances) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Hematology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
