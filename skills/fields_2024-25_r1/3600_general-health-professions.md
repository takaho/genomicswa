# General Health Professions: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current General Health Professions article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access General Health Professions articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Sentence length: more than other fields** — target about 26 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: join closely related short sentences with subordinate clauses (which, whereas, because).

Typical introduction phrasing in General Health Professions (use where it fits the content): "There is …"; "This paper …"; "While there …"; "Despite the …"; "At the …"; "The study …"; "Thus there …"; "In other …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.6 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".

Typical discussion phrasing in General Health Professions (use where it fits the content): "The findings …"; "Future research …"; "This was …"; "Limitations and …"; "The study …"; "However most …"; "This paper …"; "This research …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (enhance, additionally, crucial, insights, comprehensive, enhancing, notably, fostering, robust, foster) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in General Health Professions; do not exceed that.

The stressors that dental health care professionals are under include working under time and academic pressures, coping with difficult patients, medical emergencies and dissatisfied patients, management/staff issues, clinical and nonclinical paperwork and fear of complaints/litigation [ ]."
- Discussion (PMC12365625, Journal of Genetic Counseling, 2025): "This study aimed to explore the experience of communication in healthcare settings with parents of children with rare genetic conditions (diagnosed and undiagnosed). Parents were invited to share both positive and negative experiences of communication with GHCPs and N‐GHCPs to allow for a broad and thorough understanding of current communication practices in Australia. This study found that inclusive, clear, and respectful communication that incorporated simple, tailored explanations helped alleviate stress and enhance understanding."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
