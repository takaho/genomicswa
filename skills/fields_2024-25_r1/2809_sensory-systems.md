# Sensory Systems: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Sensory Systems article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Sensory Systems articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.72 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.5 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical introduction phrasing in Sensory Systems (use where it fits the content): "In both …"; "To investigate …"; "Although the …"; "This finding …"; "We found …"; "If the …"; "Here we …"; "While the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.7 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").

Typical discussion phrasing in Sensory Systems (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "This conclusion …"; "Nevertheless it …"; "We demonstrate …"; "A previous …"; "Our results …"; "Indeed the …"; "Thus there …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, robust, enhance, enhances, elucidate, enhancing) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Sensory Systems; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
