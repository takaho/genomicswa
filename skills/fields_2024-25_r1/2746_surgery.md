# Surgery: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Surgery article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Surgery articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical introduction phrasing in Surgery (use where it fits the content): "In our …"; "The aim …"; "This study …"; "Despite the …"; "Therefore we …"; "Therefore the …"; "As the …"; "However the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical discussion phrasing in Surgery (use where it fits the content): "In our …"; "There was …"; "They found …"; "In such …"; "Several studies …"; "In their …"; "Additionally in …"; "This can …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, insights, enhancing, robust, enhances, elucidate) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Surgery; do not exceed that.

- Introduction (PMC10845590, Diabetology & Metabolic Syndrome, 2024): "MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are short RNAs (20–22 nucleotides) that do not code for proteins and have been recognized over the past decade as important gene expression regulators [ ]. Nie et al. discovered a significant reduction in the miRNA-497 expression level in the dermis of diabetic mice, and miRNA-497 intradermal injection around whole dermal wounds in diabetic mice decreased the production of representative proinflammatory cytokines, such as tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and effectively accelerated wound closure [ ]."
- Discussion (PMC12499805, Medicine, 2025): "Garofalo et al[ ] studied a cohort of 18 patients with IRCT and an associated tear or dehiscence of the anterior and middle deltoid muscle. Among these patients, 4 had iatrogenic tears from previous surgery, while 14 had attritional deltoid tears. All patients underwent RTSA via a modified anterosuperior approach. The anterior and middle fibers of the deltoid were mobilized and sutured back to their origin on the acromion using a side-to-side and transosseous sutures. The outcomes demonstrated significant improvements in ROM."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
