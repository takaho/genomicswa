# Small Animals: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Small Animals article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Small Animals articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").

Typical introduction phrasing in Small Animals (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "In one …"; "To the …"; "The second …"; "To our …"; "Other studies …"; "Despite this …"; "In a …"; "…in one study…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.

Typical discussion phrasing in Small Animals (use where it fits the content): "Of the …"; "In the …"; "To the …"; "Further studies …"; "However with …"; "However in …"; "As previously …"; "There were …"
