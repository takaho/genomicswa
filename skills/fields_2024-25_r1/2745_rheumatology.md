# Rheumatology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Rheumatology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Rheumatology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.

Typical introduction phrasing in Rheumatology (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "They have …"; "In this …"; "However the …"; "Despite this …"; "Given the …"; "In addition …"; "The most …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical discussion phrasing in Rheumatology (use where it fits the content): "Further studies …"; "In our …"; "At present …"; "Our study …"; "In addition …"; "Although no …"; "Although a …"; "However given …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, robust, elucidate, pivotal, enhancing) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Rheumatology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
