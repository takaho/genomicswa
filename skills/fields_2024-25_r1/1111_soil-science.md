# Soil Science: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Soil Science article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Soil Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 24.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.70 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.

Typical introduction phrasing in Soil Science (use where it fits the content): "Therefore it …"; "They found …"; "The research …"; "However the …"; "However most …"; "Moreover the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 23.6 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.69 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.

Typical discussion phrasing in Soil Science (use where it fits the content): "Effects of …"; "This indicates …"; "This is …"; "Effect of …"; "This can …"; "The addition …"; "At the …"; "Overall the …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (enhance, additionally, enhancing, crucial, notably, enhances, comprehensive, insights, pivotal, robust) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Soil Science; do not exceed that.

The tight clustering of these fiber-related traits near the origin suggests minimal variability in nutrient composition and fiber fractions, reflecting uniform responses to the organic treatments."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
