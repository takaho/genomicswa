# Physiology (medical): research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Physiology (medical) article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Physiology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Additive connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.

Typical introduction phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits the content): "Therefore the …"; "One of …"; "Therefore this …"; "Some studies …"; "It is …"; "Therefore we …"; "The aim …"; "Moreover the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical discussion phrasing in Physiology (medical) (use where it fits the content): "A previous …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "Although no …"; "Moreover we …"; "Indeed in …"; "Finally this …"; "However no …"; "We also …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, comprehensive, enhance, insights, robust, enhancing, elucidate, enhances) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Physiology (medical); do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
