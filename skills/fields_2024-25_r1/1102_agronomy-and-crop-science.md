# Agronomy and Crop Science: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Agronomy and Crop Science article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Agronomy and Crop Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Aim statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit aim formulas: "Here, we…", "In this study…", "We aimed to…", "To investigate/determine/assess…".

Typical introduction phrasing in Agronomy and Crop Science (use where it fits the content): "Moreover the …"; "To the …"; "On the …"; "The study …"; "At present …"; "It has …"; "Some of …"; "Furthermore the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.69 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical discussion phrasing in Agronomy and Crop Science (use where it fits the content): "The results …"; "At the …"; "The effect …"; "Effects of …"; "Effect of …"; "They are …"; "This paper …"; "This indicates …"
