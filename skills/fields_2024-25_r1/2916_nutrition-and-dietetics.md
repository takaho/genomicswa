# Nutrition and Dietetics: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Nutrition and Dietetics article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Nutrition and Dietetics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical introduction phrasing in Nutrition and Dietetics (use where it fits the content): "A study …"; "Other studies …"; "Previous research …"; "This study …"; "Therefore this …"; "In a …"; "Our previous …"; "In addition …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.

Typical discussion phrasing in Nutrition and Dietetics (use where it fits the content): "The finding …"; "The result …"; "The possible …"; "This finding …"; "The study …"; "It has …"; "This study …"; "The findings …"; "…was in line with…"; "…was in line…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, enhance, notably, insights, enhancing, comprehensive, enhances, robust, elucidate) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Nutrition and Dietetics; do not exceed that.
