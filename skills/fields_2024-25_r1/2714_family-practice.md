# Family Practice: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Family Practice article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Family Practice articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 26.5 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.

Typical introduction phrasing in Family Practice (use where it fits the content): "The who …"; "There are …"; "The number …"; "The aim …"; "A study …"; "A recent …"; "It is …"; "Despite the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 26.3 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.

Typical discussion phrasing in Family Practice (use where it fits the content): "This study …"; "This can …"; "This was …"; "The findings …"; "Future research …"; "We were …"; "Limitations the …"; "This has …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, enhance, comprehensive, crucial, insights, enhancing, notably, underscores, multifaceted, robust) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Family Practice; do not exceed that.

The availability of MS medications exclusively at hospitals and their monthly dosing regimens likely facilitated adherence by reducing logistical barriers. This finding aligns with prior studies demonstrating the role of accessible healthcare systems in supporting adherence [ ]. The higher adherence observed among patients receiving monthly/biannually injectable medications may be explained by the hospital protocol, where these treatments are administered directly in the hospital under medical supervision."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
