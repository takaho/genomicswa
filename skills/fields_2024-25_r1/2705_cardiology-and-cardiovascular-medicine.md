# Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Additive connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical introduction phrasing in Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine (use where it fits the content): "We aimed …"; "The aim …"; "There is …"; "The study …"; "Therefore this …"; "Given the …"; "Our study …"; "However the …"
