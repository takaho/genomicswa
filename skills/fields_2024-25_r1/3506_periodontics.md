# Periodontics: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Periodontics article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Periodontics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.97 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical introduction phrasing in Periodontics (use where it fits the content): "It has …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore the …"; "Hence the …"; "It is …"; "One of …"; "Many studies …"; "Moreover the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.82 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.

Typical discussion phrasing in Periodontics (use where it fits the content): "The present …"; "Notably a …"; "Within the …"; "Recent studies …"; "A study …"; "Future studies …"; "However further …"; "In previous …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, enhance, comprehensive, insights, enhancing, elucidate, robust, enhances) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Periodontics; do not exceed that.
