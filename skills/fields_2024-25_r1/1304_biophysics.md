# Biophysics: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Biophysics article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Biophysics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.

Typical introduction phrasing in Biophysics (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "We demonstrate …"; "In particular …"; "In this …"; "Finally we …"; "However these …"; "One of …"; "Our results …"; "…we demonstrate the…"; "…we present a…"; "…this work we…"; "…in this work we…"; "…as shown in…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 2.9 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.72 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.

Typical discussion phrasing in Biophysics (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "We have …"; "We demonstrate …"; "Future work …"; "We demonstrated …"; "This work …"; "For the …"; "We then …"; "…in this work…"; "…in this work we…"; "…this work we…"; "…we present a…"

3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
