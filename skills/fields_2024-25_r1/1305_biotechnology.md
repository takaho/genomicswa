# Biotechnology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Biotechnology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Biotechnology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.

Typical introduction phrasing in Biotechnology (use where it fits the content): "As a …"; "In this …"; "One of …"; "Moreover the …"; "They are …"; "Previously we …"; "To the …"; "They can …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.

Typical discussion phrasing in Biotechnology (use where it fits the content): "This work …"; "To further …"; "However further …"; "Here we …"; "It has …"; "We have …"; "In line …"; "Overall this …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, enhance, notably, crucial, enhancing, robust, insights, enhances, comprehensive, elucidate) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Biotechnology; do not exceed that.

The optimum reaction pH and temperature of purified Cel5A were 5.0 and 50 °C, respectively; the enzyme exhibited excellent thermal stability across a pH range of 5.0–9.0 and a temperature range of 20–60 °C. However, the pure enzyme solution may be denatured due to its sensitivity to high temperatures, and Cel5A was almost inactivated when the temperature increased to 70 °C."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
