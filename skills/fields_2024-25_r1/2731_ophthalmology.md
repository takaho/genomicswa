# Ophthalmology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Ophthalmology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Ophthalmology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.74 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").

Typical introduction phrasing in Ophthalmology (use where it fits the content): "At present …"; "As the …"; "It has …"; "There are …"; "This study …"; "The aim …"; "In our …"; "A study …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.57 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").

Typical discussion phrasing in Ophthalmology (use where it fits the content): "In our …"; "They reported …"; "In conclusion …"; "They found …"; "In previous …"; "A previous …"; "Because the …"; "Several studies …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, insights, comprehensive, enhance, enhancing, robust, elucidate, enhances) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Ophthalmology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
