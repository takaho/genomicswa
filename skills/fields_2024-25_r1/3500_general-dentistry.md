# General Dentistry: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current General Dentistry article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access General Dentistry articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in General Dentistry (use where it fits the content): "Therefore the …"; "The aim …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "It has …"; "This in …"; "Additionally the …"; "They are …"; "There are …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.

Typical discussion phrasing in General Dentistry (use where it fits the content): "Within the …"; "The present …"; "The findings …"; "A previous …"; "The results …"; "They reported …"; "The study …"; "For this …"; "…within the limitations of…"; "…within the limitations…"

## Procedure

2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
