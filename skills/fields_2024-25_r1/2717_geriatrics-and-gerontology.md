# Geriatrics and Gerontology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Geriatrics and Gerontology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Geriatrics and Gerontology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.

Typical introduction phrasing in Geriatrics and Gerontology (use where it fits the content): "The who …"; "During the …"; "Research on …"; "There is …"; "The number …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore we …"; "With the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.

Typical discussion phrasing in Geriatrics and Gerontology (use where it fits the content): "This study …"; "In such …"; "Our findings …"; "Data from …"; "It may …"; "Other studies …"; "Additionally it …"; "Finally our …"
