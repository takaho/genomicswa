# Endocrinology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Endocrinology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Endocrinology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical introduction phrasing in Endocrinology (use where it fits the content): "In addition …"; "We have …"; "Finally we …"; "Our results …"; "Here we …"; "Some of …"; "The present …"; "Additionally it …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.

Typical discussion phrasing in Endocrinology (use where it fits the content): "Our data …"; "Here we …"; "We show …"; "These data …"; "This work …"; "We observed …"; "Indeed our …"; "We have …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, enhance, insights, comprehensive, robust, enhancing, enhances, elucidate) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Endocrinology; do not exceed that.
