# Infectious Diseases: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Infectious Diseases article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Infectious Diseases articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 2.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.0 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Sentence length: more than other fields** — target about 26 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: join closely related short sentences with subordinate clauses (which, whereas, because).

Typical introduction phrasing in Infectious Diseases (use where it fits the content): "As of …"; "During the …"; "Since then …"; "In the …"; "The number …"; "At the …"; "We also …"; "Most of …"; "…was reported in…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.5 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.

Typical discussion phrasing in Infectious Diseases (use where it fits the content): "The overall …"; "This finding …"; "Other studies …"; "This was …"; "With the …"; "Findings from …"; "A study …"; "In a …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, robust, enhancing, underscores, underscore) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Infectious Diseases; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
