# Health Informatics: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Health Informatics article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Health Informatics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.74 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.1 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Aim statements: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: state the aim explicitly: "Here, we…", "In this study…", "We aimed to…", "To investigate/determine/assess…".
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Health Informatics (use where it fits the content): "This study …"; "While these …"; "Through this …"; "In other …"; "The recent …"; "Specifically we …"; "Moreover the …"; "Despite these …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").

Typical discussion phrasing in Health Informatics (use where it fits the content): "Future research …"; "Future work …"; "Second while …"; "The potential …"; "First the …"; "While the …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, enhance, comprehensive, crucial, insights, enhancing, notably, robust, underscores, nuanced) occur about 1.4 times per 300 words in Health Informatics; do not exceed that.

## Style illustration (verbatim, CC BY; do not copy content)
- Introduction (PMC11108146, PLoS ONE, 2024): "Manually curating genomic repositories and annotating genetic relationships from literature is time-consuming and challenging [ ]. Traditional reading and summarising lack scalability, are subject to personal interpretations, and are time-consuming. Automating the pre-processing of scientific literature and post-processing of extracted information using Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques can address these limitations. Several researchers have studied automating pre-processing and post-processing for accurately extracting relations from unstructured data. Most pre-processing techniques involve the extraction of all available abstracts within a specific timeline from online resources such as PubMed [ ]."
- Discussion (PMC12023801, JMIR Medical Informatics, 2025): "The timing of AI advice relative to the user’s decision may influence the user’s performance and trust. Gajos and Mamykina [ ] recently examined AI advice timing in 3 conditions: (1) AI recommendation and explanation before making a decision, (2) AI recommendation and explanation after making a decision, and (3) AI explanation only before making a decision. While all 3 conditions led to improved decision accuracy, only the third condition showed improvement in both decision accuracy and learning gain."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
