# Epidemiology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Epidemiology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Epidemiology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.72 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 2.0 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.

Typical introduction phrasing in Epidemiology (use where it fits the content): "The who …"; "During the …"; "As of …"; "To this …"; "However some …"; "As such …"; "In a …"; "Given the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.

Typical discussion phrasing in Epidemiology (use where it fits the content): "The second …"; "We were …"; "The overall …"; "Results from …"; "In these …"; "Our findings …"; "This would …"; "A second …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, comprehensive, enhance, insights, robust, enhancing, underscores, underscore) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Epidemiology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
