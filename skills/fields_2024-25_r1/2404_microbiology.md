# Microbiology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Microbiology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Microbiology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical introduction phrasing in Microbiology (use where it fits the content): "The most …"; "We have …"; "Most of …"; "We also …"; "However to …"; "In our …"; "Our previous …"; "Moreover the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical discussion phrasing in Microbiology (use where it fits the content): "A previous …"; "From to …"; "We previously …"; "Future work …"; "We were …"; "However a …"; "This was …"; "We have …"

Bacterial biofilms formed on the surface of medical devices make the infection inherently more difficult to treat [ ]. ## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
