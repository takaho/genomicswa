# Food Science: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Food Science article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Food Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").

Typical introduction phrasing in Food Science (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "During the …"; "Moreover the …"; "In addition …"; "For the …"; "Therefore the …"; "However they …"; "It has …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.81 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.

Typical discussion phrasing in Food Science (use where it fits the content): "Conclusion in …"; "Conclusion this …"; "The essential …"; "Overall the …"; "The other …"; "In particular …"; "Moreover the …"; "It was …"

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
