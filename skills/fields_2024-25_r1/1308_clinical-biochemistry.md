# Clinical Biochemistry: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Clinical Biochemistry article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Clinical Biochemistry articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 per 300 words (all fields about 0.1 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".

Typical introduction phrasing in Clinical Biochemistry (use where it fits the content): "There are …"; "In our …"; "In the …"; "Therefore we …"; "For this …"; "The most …"; "We also …"; "Furthermore we …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.8 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").

Typical discussion phrasing in Clinical Biochemistry (use where it fits the content): "The other …"; "Although some …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, elucidate, enhancing, enhances, robust) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Clinical Biochemistry; do not exceed that.

Increased 2‐HG excretion does not occur in GA1, but is a characteristic finding in several other neurometabolic disorders collectively known as 2‐hydroxyglutaric acidurias. Elevated 2‐HG excretion can also result from disorders of riboflavin metabolism, notably including multiple acyl‐CoA dehydrogenase deficiency (MADD), also known as glutaric aciduria type 2."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
