# Orthodontics: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Orthodontics article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Orthodontics articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 24.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Booster adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: clearly, indeed, strongly, markedly, dramatically, remarkably.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Orthodontics (use where it fits the content): "Therefore the …"; "The aim …"; "One of …"; "Recently a …"; "On the …"; "For this …"; "Therefore this …"; "Hence the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.8 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.69 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.1 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Gap/novelty statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: avoid gap/novelty formulas: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.

Typical discussion phrasing in Orthodontics (use where it fits the content): "In the …"; "This can …"; "The present …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore in …"; "The findings …"; "The results …"; "This was …"

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
