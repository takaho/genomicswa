# Genetics (clinical): research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Genetics (clinical) article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Genetics (clinical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.69 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Causal connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result.

Typical introduction phrasing in Genetics (clinical) (use where it fits the content): "This results …"; "We previously …"; "In the …"; "The most …"; "Here we …"; "The aim …"; "Furthermore we …"; "There is …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.60 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.

Typical discussion phrasing in Genetics (clinical) (use where it fits the content): "In a …"; "In our …"; "The most …"; "There was …"; "Our data …"; "There are …"; "Analysis of …"; "As such …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, robust, enhancing, underscores, landscape) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Genetics (clinical); do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
