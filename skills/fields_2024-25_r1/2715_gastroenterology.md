# Gastroenterology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Gastroenterology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Gastroenterology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".

Typical introduction phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits the content): "Therefore we …"; "Although the …"; "Notably the …"; "Therefore there …"; "This study …"; "Given the …"; "The results …"; "Our findings …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions.

Typical discussion phrasing in Gastroenterology (use where it fits the content): "In our …"; "First this …"; "To our …"; "In conclusion …"; "There was …"; "Our study …"; "Although there …"; "In a …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, insights, comprehensive, enhance, enhancing, enhances, elucidate, robust) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Gastroenterology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
