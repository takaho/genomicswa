# Psychiatry and Mental health: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Psychiatry and Mental health article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Psychiatry and Mental health articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Psychiatry and Mental health (use where it fits the content): "One study …"; "Given the …"; "Despite the …"; "A recent …"; "On the …"; "Some studies …"; "There is …"; "Furthermore a …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.6 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.

Typical discussion phrasing in Psychiatry and Mental health (use where it fits the content): "Our findings …"; "Future research …"; "To our …"; "Future studies …"; "This finding …"; "The findings …"; "Further the …"; "Our finding …"

## Procedure

2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
