# Cancer Research: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Cancer Research article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Cancer Research articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.

Typical introduction phrasing in Cancer Research (use where it fits the content): "In this …"; "We present …"; "Research has …"; "In our …"; "However the …"; "It has …"; "Additionally we …"; "Recent studies …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Additive connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.5 per 300 words). How: use more of: moreover, furthermore, in addition, also, similarly, likewise.

Typical discussion phrasing in Cancer Research (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "The role …"; "To further …"; "Moreover a …"; "In summary …"; "To investigate …"; "In conclusion …"; "These data …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, insights, comprehensive, enhance, enhancing, enhances, pivotal, elucidate) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Cancer Research; do not exceed that.

## Style illustration (verbatim, CC BY; do not copy content)
- Introduction (PMC11152612, Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal, 2024): "Today, cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide and presents a significant challenge for the healthcare system . To uncover the critical genomic and genetic changes in cancer, it is essential to explore various aspects, including genomic alterations, methylation patterns, and changes in mRNA and microRNA (miRNA) expression levels. MiRNAs play a crucial role in carcinogenesis by binding to mRNAs and consequently influencing the protein profile of the cell ."
- Discussion (PMC12472857, Pharmaceuticals, 2025): "Recently, several studies have identified the important role of miR-25-3p in various cancers; however, the specific role of this miRNA in UBC and its associated mechanisms remain unclear. A KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the predicted target genes for miR-25-3p highlighted key pathways related to bladder cancer and platinum resistance, emphasizing the importance of this miRNA in the context of UBC. GO demonstrated its essential role in cancer-related cellular pathways, including cell migration, proliferation, adhesion, and oxidative stress."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
