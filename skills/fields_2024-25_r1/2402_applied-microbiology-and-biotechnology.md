# Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.

## Discussion: what to change

- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.88 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.

Typical discussion phrasing in Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology (use where it fits the content): "In this …"; "In summary …"; "Conclusion in …"; "This work …"; "Here we …"; "However further …"; "Overall this …"; "Interestingly the …"
