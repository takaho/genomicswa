# Cognitive Neuroscience: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Cognitive Neuroscience article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Cognitive Neuroscience articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.

Typical introduction phrasing in Cognitive Neuroscience (use where it fits the content): "In other …"; "That is …"; "Taken together …"; "With this …"; "A more …"; "Finally we …"; "In contrast …"; "These two …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Sentence length: more than other fields** — target about 26 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: join closely related short sentences with subordinate clauses (which, whereas, because).
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.

Typical discussion phrasing in Cognitive Neuroscience (use where it fits the content): "Future work …"; "Our results …"; "As such …"; "In line …"; "Limitations and …"; "In particular …"; "Future studies …"; "It would …"

- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, robust, enhance, insights, comprehensive, enhancing, enhances, underscore) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Cognitive Neuroscience; do not exceed that.

- Discussion (PMC11838891, PLoS Computational Biology, 2025): "Identification of different dynamical regimes with potentially similar timescales can be obscured by artifacts that arise from either too fine or too coarse sampling. Conceptually, the autoregressive fitting of stochastic dynamics lies between two extremes. Over infinitesimal time delays, the autoregressive matrix approaches the identity and the error approaches zero. On the other hand, for infinitely long delays the autoregressive matrix approaches zero and the prediction error approaches the variance of the data (R2 = 0)."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
