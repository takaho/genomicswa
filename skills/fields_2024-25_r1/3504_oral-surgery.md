# Oral Surgery: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Oral Surgery article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Oral Surgery articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.1 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result.

Typical introduction phrasing in Oral Surgery (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "To the …"; "Therefore the …"; "It has …"; "Therefore this …"; "The second …"; "Thus the …"; "Therefore it …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 23 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Summary connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.

Typical discussion phrasing in Oral Surgery (use where it fits the content): "Within the …"; "The present …"; "In the …"; "This result …"; "This can …"; "The results …"; "They reported …"; "It was …"; "…within the limitations of…"; "…within the limitations…"
