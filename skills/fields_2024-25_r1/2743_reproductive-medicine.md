# Reproductive Medicine: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Reproductive Medicine article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Reproductive Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.

Typical introduction phrasing in Reproductive Medicine (use where it fits the content): "It has …"; "During the …"; "Several studies …"; "In our …"; "Furthermore it …"; "It is …"; "It also …"; "Therefore the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.6 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").

Typical discussion phrasing in Reproductive Medicine (use where it fits the content): "Studies have …"; "To the …"; "Although there …"; "It has …"; "Some studies …"; "In our …"; "We observed …"; "To further …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, enhance, insights, comprehensive, enhancing, enhances, pivotal, elucidate) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Reproductive Medicine; do not exceed that.

3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
