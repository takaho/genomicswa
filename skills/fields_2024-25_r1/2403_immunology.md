# Immunology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Immunology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Immunology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Aim statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit aim formulas: "Here, we…", "In this study…", "We aimed to…", "To investigate/determine/assess…".
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical introduction phrasing in Immunology (use where it fits the content): "The role …"; "We further …"; "In this …"; "Here we …"; "However the …"; "Notably the …"; "We previously …"; "Collectively these …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.6 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".

Typical discussion phrasing in Immunology (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "Our data …"; "Thus we …"; "We previously …"; "Additionally it …"; "Furthermore we …"; "We have …"; "Thus in …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, enhance, enhancing, robust, insights, enhances, comprehensive, elucidate) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Immunology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure

3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
