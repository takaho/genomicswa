# Otorhinolaryngology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Otorhinolaryngology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Otorhinolaryngology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.
- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Hedging adverbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they.

Typical introduction phrasing in Otorhinolaryngology (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "There are …"; "In the …"; "Moreover the …"; "Therefore we …"; "Despite its …"; "Recent studies …"; "It also …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Sentence length: less than other fields** — target about 24 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: split long sentences; one idea per sentence.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Summary connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: together, collectively, taken together, overall, in summary, in conclusion.

Typical discussion phrasing in Otorhinolaryngology (use where it fits the content): "In such …"; "In our …"; "Furthermore there …"; "Our study …"; "Therefore we …"; "The role …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore a …"
