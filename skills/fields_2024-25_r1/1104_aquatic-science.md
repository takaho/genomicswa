# Aquatic Science: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Aquatic Science article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Aquatic Science articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 17.8 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.

Typical introduction phrasing in Aquatic Science (use where it fits the content): "The findings …"; "In addition …"; "Studies on …"; "They also …"; "They are …"; "Thus the …"; "Previous studies …"; "Research on …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.7 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.

Typical discussion phrasing in Aquatic Science (use where it fits the content): "In this …"; "Similar results …"; "These results …"; "Effects of …"; "Effect of …"; "Studies on …"; "This indicates …"; "Research has …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, enhance, crucial, notably, enhancing, insights, comprehensive, enhances, robust, pivotal) occur about 0.9 times per 300 words in Aquatic Science; do not exceed that.
