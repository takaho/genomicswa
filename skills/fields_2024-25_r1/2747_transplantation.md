# Transplantation: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Transplantation article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Transplantation articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.0 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Transplantation (use where it fits the content): "The first …"; "The aim …"; "Therefore in …"; "The current …"; "In our …"; "Despite the …"; "In a …"; "Our study …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Contrast connectives: more than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.

Typical discussion phrasing in Transplantation (use where it fits the content): "In our …"; "Our study …"; "In a …"; "In conclusion …"; "Because of …"; "We did …"; "At present …"; "There was …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, comprehensive, crucial, enhance, insights, robust, enhancing, pivotal, elucidate) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Transplantation; do not exceed that.

## Procedure

2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
