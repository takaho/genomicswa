# Parasitology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Parasitology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Parasitology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 17.3 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.

Typical introduction phrasing in Parasitology (use where it fits the content): "They have …"; "The first …"; "Some of …"; "Although the …"; "However in …"; "Here we …"; "It also …"; "Thus the …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Passive voice: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: report procedures and established facts in the passive ("X was observed", "Y has been reported").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.1 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.

Typical discussion phrasing in Parasitology (use where it fits the content): "Similar findings …"; "Studies of …"; "As previously …"; "Our results …"; "In contrast …"; "Thus in …"

## Procedure

2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
