# Speech and Hearing: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Speech and Hearing article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Speech and Hearing articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.71 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.

Typical introduction phrasing in Speech and Hearing (use where it fits the content): "The aim …"; "As such …"; "However no …"; "There is …"; "In a …"; "However to …"; "The present …"; "Other studies …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **"It is…"/"There are…" constructions: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: use "It is … that" and "There is/are …" constructions.
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Importance words: less than other fields** — target about 0.7 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".

Typical discussion phrasing in Speech and Hearing (use where it fits the content): "There was …"; "Further research …"; "That is …"; "They found …"; "This can …"; "There are …"; "The present …"; "In future …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, comprehensive, enhance, crucial, notably, insights, robust, enhancing, underscore, enhances) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Speech and Hearing; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
