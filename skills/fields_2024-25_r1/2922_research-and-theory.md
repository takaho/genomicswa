# Research and Theory: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Research and Theory article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Research and Theory articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.70 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.

Typical introduction phrasing in Research and Theory (use where it fits the content): "Therefore this …"; "A study …"; "In other …"; "Previous research …"; "This was …"; "As a …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.

Typical discussion phrasing in Research and Theory (use where it fits the content): "The findings …"; "Future research …"; "The study …"; "The results …"; "This can …"; "Limitations the …"; "Limitations this …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (enhance, additionally, crucial, comprehensive, enhancing, insights, fostering, robust, foster, enhances) occur about 1.1 times per 300 words in Research and Theory; do not exceed that.

Confucius once said, “Honest advice may be unpleasant, but it benefits actions,” and during the feudal era, institutions such as the Imperial Court of Censors were established, reflecting the cultural tradition of offering advice. With the rapid development of the global socioeconomic landscape and changes in organizational environments, the significance and scope of voice behavior have been continuously emphasized. Internationally, the concept of voice behavior originated from the EVI model proposed by American economist Hirschman [ ]."
- Discussion (PMC11587416, Nursing Reports, 2024): "The study’s findings highlight a critical issue in the management of coronary heart disease (CHD) among the Minangkabau ethnic group in West Sumatra, Indonesia. The research revealed that patients, their families, and even Indigenous leaders demonstrated inadequate knowledge about CHD and struggled to identify its early symptoms. This lack of understanding was consistent across all three groups, regardless of their education levels. Patients often misinterpreted CHD symptoms, attributing them to less serious conditions like gastritis or colds."

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
