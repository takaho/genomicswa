# Pharmacology (medical): research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Pharmacology (medical) article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Pharmacology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 19.1 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmacology (medical) (use where it fits the content): "The most …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "In this …"; "Moreover it …"; "In a …"; "Most of …"; "In addition …"; "Many studies …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.4 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmacology (medical) (use where it fits the content): "In particular …"; "Thus these …"; "They are …"; "Our results …"; "These limitations …"; "In previous …"; "Therefore the …"; "The results …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, enhance, crucial, insights, comprehensive, enhancing, robust, elucidate, enhances) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Pharmacology (medical); do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
