# Internal Medicine: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Internal Medicine article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Internal Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Aim statements: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: state the aim explicitly: "Here, we…", "In this study…", "We aimed to…", "To investigate/determine/assess…".
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Internal Medicine (use where it fits the content): "However there …"; "Several studies …"; "The aim …"; "In a …"; "This study …"; "Therefore we …"; "Despite these …"; "There are …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Past tense: more than other fields** — target about 4.7 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: report earlier studies and this study's observations in the past tense.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.

Typical discussion phrasing in Internal Medicine (use where it fits the content): "Study limitations …"; "To our …"; "Our study …"; "In our …"; "In a …"; "We were …"; "Nevertheless we …"; "In such …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, robust, insights, underscores, enhancing, elucidate) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Internal Medicine; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
