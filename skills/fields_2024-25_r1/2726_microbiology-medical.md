# Microbiology (medical): research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Microbiology (medical) article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Microbiology (medical) articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 6.01 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.8 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.

Typical introduction phrasing in Microbiology (medical) (use where it fits the content): "The increasing …"; "Moreover the …"; "Most of …"; "Since then …"; "The aim …"; "Notably the …"

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
