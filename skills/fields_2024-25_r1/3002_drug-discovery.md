# Drug Discovery: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Drug Discovery article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Drug Discovery articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Vocabulary variety: more than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.73 (all fields MATTR about 0.72). How: vary wording; avoid repeating the same noun or verb in neighbouring sentences.

Typical introduction phrasing in Drug Discovery (use where it fits the content): "In this …"; "For the …"; "One of …"; "However these …"; "Furthermore the …"; "Finally the …"; "This work …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Gap/novelty statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt implies a gap, state it with: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.88 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.

Typical discussion phrasing in Drug Discovery (use where it fits the content): "We then …"; "Specifically we …"; "As shown …"; "This work …"; "This research …"; "In this …"; "From the …"; "Moreover a …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, insights, notably, comprehensive, enhance, robust, enhancing, enhances, pivotal) occur about 1.2 times per 300 words in Drug Discovery; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
