# Hepatology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Hepatology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Hepatology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **This/these/they as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: repeat the noun instead of this/it/they.

Typical introduction phrasing in Hepatology (use where it fits the content): "Recent studies …"; "Nevertheless the …"; "The aim …"; "However the …"; "However there …"; "Moreover the …"; "As such …"; "There are …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 2.2 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Hedging adverbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 per 300 words (all fields about 1.0 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: likely, possibly, probably, potentially, generally, relatively, largely, often.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").

Typical discussion phrasing in Hepatology (use where it fits the content): "Therefore we …"; "Our study …"; "In conclusion …"; "In our …"; "Second we …"; "However its …"; "In recent …"; "To further …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, insights, robust, enhancing, pivotal, elucidate) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Hepatology; do not exceed that.

This study represents, to the authors' knowledge, the most extensive real-world study of liver cancer screening conducted thus far. The screening approach is characterized by its dual-phase structure. The initial screening utilizes solely gender and three serum markers (AST, GGT, TBA) for preliminary assessment, whereas the subsequent phase incorporates an additional serum marker, AFP, for further evaluation."
