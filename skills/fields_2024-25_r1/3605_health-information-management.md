# Health Information Management: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Health Information Management article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Health Information Management articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.0 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.1 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Contrast connectives: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.2 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: however, although, whereas, in contrast, nevertheless, despite.

Typical introduction phrasing in Health Information Management (use where it fits the content): "The data …"; "The research …"; "This research …"; "It also …"; "This study …"; "…of the paper…"; "…the paper is…"; "…of this paper…"; "…for future research…"; "…of the data…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: less than other fields** — target about 5.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we", the study or the finding the subject instead of the entity.
- **Future-work statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: phrase open questions as: "Future studies…", "Further research is needed…".
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Booster verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.5 per 300 words (all fields about 1.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.

Typical discussion phrasing in Health Information Management (use where it fits the content): "Future work …"; "This research …"; "To address …"; "This study …"; "Limitations and …"; "The research …"; "…data such as…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (enhance, additionally, comprehensive, crucial, insights, robust, enhancing, notably, enhances, underscores) occur about 1.4 times per 300 words in Health Information Management; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
