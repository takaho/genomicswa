# Structural Biology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Structural Biology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Structural Biology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.67 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Modal hedges: less than other fields** — target about 0.6 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: may, might, could, would.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 18.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").

Typical introduction phrasing in Structural Biology (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "If the …"; "In this …"; "The first …"; "For the …"; "This is …"; "We have …"; "With this …"; "…have been determined…"; "…we report the…"; "…is the number of…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 2.2 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Sentence length: more than other fields** — target about 27 words per sentence (all fields about 25 words per sentence). How: join closely related short sentences with subordinate clauses (which, whereas, because).
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".

Typical discussion phrasing in Structural Biology (use where it fits the content): "This work …"; "We have …"; "As shown …"; "In particular …"; "Here we …"; "When the …"; "In summary …"; "If the …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, insights, notably, crucial, enhance, robust, comprehensive, enhancing, enhances, elucidate) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Structural Biology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure

2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
