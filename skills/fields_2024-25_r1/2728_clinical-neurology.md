# Clinical Neurology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Clinical Neurology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Clinical Neurology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use more of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 1.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.9 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.

Typical introduction phrasing in Clinical Neurology (use where it fits the content): "We also …"; "However these …"; "In this …"; "It has …"; "Finally we …"; "However to …"; "Previous studies …"; "Therefore this …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Modal hedges: more than other fields** — target about 2.4 per 300 words (all fields about 2.0 per 300 words). How: use more of: may, might, could, would.
- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.

Typical discussion phrasing in Clinical Neurology (use where it fits the content): "A previous …"; "Both studies …"; "In line …"; "Our results …"; "These data …"; "We showed …"; "They are …"; "Although we …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhance, robust, enhancing, elucidate, pivotal) occur about 0.8 times per 300 words in Clinical Neurology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
