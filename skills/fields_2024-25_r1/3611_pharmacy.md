# Pharmacy: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Pharmacy article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Pharmacy articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.9 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.93 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.8 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.9 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Pharmacy (use where it fits the content): "A study …"; "The role …"; "The study …"; "The findings …"; "This study …"; "Therefore this …"; "In the …"; "There is …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Word length: more than other fields** — target about 5.86 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer precise technical and Latinate terms and noun phrases.
- **This/these/they as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.8 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: refer back with this/these/they/which as subjects.
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 22.4 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Passive voice: less than other fields** — target about 1.7 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 2.0 in 10 clause subjects). How: turn passives into active clauses with an explicit agent.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical discussion phrasing in Pharmacy (use where it fits the content): "The study …"; "The findings …"; "This study …"; "This finding …"; "More than …"; "Similar findings …"; "This was …"; "Limitations the …"; "…reported that they…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, enhance, crucial, comprehensive, insights, enhancing, notably, underscores, robust, pivotal) occur about 1.1 times per 300 words in Pharmacy; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
