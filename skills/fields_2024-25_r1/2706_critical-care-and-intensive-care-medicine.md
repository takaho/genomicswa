# Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 23.2 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.

Typical introduction phrasing in Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine (use where it fits the content): "While these …"; "Research indicates …"; "Previous studies …"; "Studies have …"; "Despite the …"; "This study …"; "However the …"; "The aim …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Limitation statements: more than other fields** — target about 0.5 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: where the excerpt mentions constraints, name them as limitations: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").
- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): more than other fields** — target about 21.8 per 300 words (all fields about 20.4 per 300 words). How: express processes as nouns ("the activation of X", "expression", "regulation").
- **Patients/participants as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make patients/participants/individuals the subject where the content allows.

Typical discussion phrasing in Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine (use where it fits the content): "Our study …"; "First this …"; "With the …"; "Future studies …"; "We did …"; "Additionally there …"; "We found …"; "These limitations …"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, notably, enhance, comprehensive, insights, robust, enhancing, underscores, enhances) occur about 1.0 times per 300 words in Critical Care and Intensive Care Medicine; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
