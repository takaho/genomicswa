# Biochemistry: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Biochemistry article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Biochemistry articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Nominalisations (-tion, -ment, -ity…): less than other fields** — target about 17.4 per 300 words (all fields about 20.2 per 300 words). How: express processes as verbs ("X activates Y").
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.6 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: make "we" the subject of what the study did or found ("We found…", "Here, we show…").
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.70 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Booster verbs: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: show, demonstrate, reveal, confirm, establish, highlight.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the finding or entity the subject instead of the method.

Typical introduction phrasing in Biochemistry (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "Our results …"; "In contrast …"; "These data …"; "These results …"; "We further …"; "In addition …"; "We found …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Past tense: less than other fields** — target about 3.2 in 10 finite verbs (all fields about 4.1 in 10 finite verbs). How: use the present tense for established knowledge and interpretation.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.54 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Vocabulary variety: less than other fields** — target MATTR about 0.69 (all fields MATTR about 0.71). How: repeat key terms consistently instead of synonyms.

Typical discussion phrasing in Biochemistry (use where it fits the content): "Here we …"; "We propose …"; "Our previous …"; "Interestingly the …"; "We have …"; "Indeed the …"; "Thus the …"; "Our data …"; "…we propose that the…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, notably, crucial, insights, enhance, comprehensive, elucidate, robust, enhances, enhancing) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Biochemistry; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
