# Animal Science and Zoology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Animal Science and Zoology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Animal Science and Zoology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.5 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 6.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.72 characters per word (all fields about 5.81 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Causal connectives: more than other fields** — target about 0.9 per 300 words (all fields about 0.7 per 300 words). How: use more of: therefore, thus, hence, consequently, because, as a result.

Typical introduction phrasing in Animal Science and Zoology (use where it fits the content): "Therefore this …"; "Therefore the …"; "Since the …"; "It has …"; "Notably the …"; "Thus the …"; "Additionally the …"; "Previous studies …"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the organism (mice, plants, strains) the subject where the content allows.
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.
- **Biological/clinical entities as subject: more than other fields** — target about 6.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 5.7 in 10 clause subjects). How: make genes, proteins, cells, compounds, diseases or variables the grammatical subjects.
- **Limitation statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid explicit limitation formulas: "A limitation of…", "should be interpreted with caution", "cannot rule out", "may not generalize".
- **"We" as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.5 in 10 clause subjects). How: avoid "we"; make the finding, entity or method the subject, or use the passive.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: less than other fields** — target about 1.1 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 1.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the entity or "we" the subject instead of data/results/study.
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.58 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.

Typical discussion phrasing in Animal Science and Zoology (use where it fits the content): "In this …"; "In the …"; "The addition …"; "Effect of …"; "The results …"; "To determine …"; "During the …"; "It has …"
