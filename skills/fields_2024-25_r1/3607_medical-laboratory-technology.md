# Medical Laboratory Technology: research-article prose style (2024–2025)

Use this to rewrite Introduction or Discussion prose so that it reads like a current Medical Laboratory Technology article. Change style only: never change numbers, statistics, citations, abbreviations, technical terms, claims or the subject matter.

Evidence: 300 open-access Medical Laboratory Technology articles (2024–2025) compared with 90 life-science fields. Targets are this field's averages; "all fields" gives the cross-field average for contrast.

## Introduction: what to change

- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Importance words: more than other fields** — target about 1.4 per 300 words (all fields about 1.1 per 300 words). How: use more of: critical, crucial, key, essential, important(ly), fundamental, vital, major.
- **Gap/novelty statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.3 per 300 words). How: avoid gap/novelty formulas: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Organisms (mice, plants, strains) as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.1 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the gene, molecule or process the subject rather than the organism.
- **Findings/data/study as subject: more than other fields** — target about 1.0 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.8 in 10 clause subjects). How: make findings/data/results/this study the subject ("These findings suggest…").

Typical introduction phrasing in Medical Laboratory Technology (use where it fits the content): "However this …"; "The aim …"; "However these …"; "In this …"; "This is …"; "Hence the …"; "There are …"; "Despite its …"; "…of the results…"

## Discussion: what to change

- **Hedging verbs: less than other fields** — target about 1.0 per 300 words (all fields about 1.3 per 300 words). How: use fewer of: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, propose.
- **Method/model/approach as subject: more than other fields** — target about 0.4 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects). How: make the method/model/approach the subject ("This approach allows…").
- **Word length: less than other fields** — target about 5.57 characters per word (all fields about 5.68 characters per word). How: prefer short plain words and verbs.
- **Gap/novelty statements: less than other fields** — target about 0.1 per 300 words (all fields about 0.2 per 300 words). How: avoid gap/novelty formulas: "remains unclear", "little is known", "poorly understood", "novel", "for the first time".
- **Patients/participants as subject: less than other fields** — target about 0.2 in 10 clause subjects (all fields about 0.3 in 10 clause subjects). How: foreground the biology or variable rather than patients/participants.

Typical discussion phrasing in Medical Laboratory Technology (use where it fits the content): "For the …"; "This can …"; "In such …"; "If the …"; "Overall the …"; "There is …"; "Therefore it …"; "There are …"; "…of the new…"

## Vocabulary
- Words often added by AI polishing (additionally, crucial, comprehensive, enhance, robust, notably, enhancing, insights, underscore, underscores) occur about 0.7 times per 300 words in Medical Laboratory Technology; do not exceed that.

## Procedure
1. Identify which of the targets above the excerpt misses.
2. Rewrite sentence by sentence, moving each of those features toward its target and leaving the rest unchanged.
3. Check that every number, [REF] marker, abbreviation and technical term is still present and that no claim was added or removed.
