Lung cancer is one of the most common malignancies worldwide and accounts for nearly 18% of cancer-related deaths, which makes it the leading cause of cancer death ( [REF] ). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) constitutes 85% of all lung cancer cases, with an average 5-year survival rate of only 10%–15% ( [REF] ). Treatment options for NSCLC include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy and targeted therapy. However, due to early metastasis, many patients are ineligible for surgery ( [REF] ). Cisplatin (DDP)-based adjuvant chemotherapy, a regimen that inflicts DNA damage, has become a primary treatment and improves 5-year overall survival by 5.4% in absolute terms ( [REF] ). Targeted therapies, such as first-generation epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) like gefitinib, are also standard treatment for advanced EGFR mutant NSCLC ( [REF] ). Despite their initial effectiveness, the clinical success of DDP and EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC is limited by acquired resistance in tumor cells ( [REF] ).

Mechanisms of resistance to drug therapy, particularly those involving DNA damage repair and apoptosis, have been studied in detail ( [REF] ). After DNA-damaging therapy, ATM (ataxia telangiectasia mutated)/ATR (ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3-related) and their downstream substrates, such as checkpoint kinase (Chk)1 and p53, are activated. These proteins directly phosphorylate members of the Cdc25 family, which prevents Cdc25 from dephosphorylating CDK1. This leads to cell-cycle arrest and DNA damage repair in tumor cells and ultimately reduces the efficacy of drugs that induce apoptosis ( [REF] ). Inhibition of DNA repair pathways can prevent cancer cells from repairing DNA damage caused by treatments such as chemotherapy or radiation, which increases cell death and improves treatment outcomes. Inhibiting Chk1 is a potential strategy for overcoming multidrug resistance (MDR) in NSCLC ( [REF] ). Preclinical and clinical studies have shown that inhibiting Chk1 signaling, alone or in combination with genotoxic chemotherapies, reduces DNA damage repair and alleviates drug resistance in several cancer models (NCT02264678, NCT01139775, NCT02589522, NCT02797964).
