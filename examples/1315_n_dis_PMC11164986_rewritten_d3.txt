For low-abundance or fragile macromolecular assemblies, and for systems with AWI-related preferred orientation in cryo-EM, affinity grids that purify target complexes on the grid directly from cell lysates offer a strong solution. Here we developed an efficient strategy based on immobilized antibody affinity grids (IAAG) (Fig. 1), which uses Pyr-NHS as a linker to anchor antibodies/Fabs or bait proteins noncovalently onto the sp2-hybridized carbon lattice of the grid surface (GO, carbon or graphene) through cross-linking with their lysine residues or N-terminus. We show that the IAAG strategy enriches target proteins (Fig. 3a, b) and overcomes preferred orientation problems (Fig. 3f–i). It also allows on-grid purification of low-abundance target complexes (Fig. 5), which simplifies purification, reduces the amount of biological sample needed and enables atomic-resolution cryo-EM (Figs. 4, 5). The IAAG strategy is an efficient and reliable approach for combined sample purification and vitrification, and it makes high-resolution cryo-EM feasible.

In contrast to existing affinity grid strategies, which mostly rely on freshly homemade graphene or GO supporting films functionalized with customized chemical reagents [REF], our IAAG strategy uses commercially available GO/carbon/graphene grids and the readily available cross-linker Pyr-NHS. Assembly of IAAG grids typically takes less than 2 hours (Fig. 1b). Consequently, the strategy is easy to use, efficient and broadly applicable, thereby making it accessible to non-specialist laboratories. We also found that GO grids treated with Pyr-NHS enriched the purified CCT6-HR sample considerably better than both the holey carbon grid and the unmodified GO grid (Supplementary Fig. 5). This shows the potential of Pyr-NHS-coated grids for immobilizing and enriching purified proteins in cryo-EM structural studies, in line with previous studies that used other Pyr derivatives for similar purposes [REF] .
